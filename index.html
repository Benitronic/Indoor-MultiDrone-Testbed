01 / THE STUDY

A closer look at
what makes flight
experiments work.

A flight result depends on more than the drone.

This study characterizes the indoor multi-drone testbed at the Aerial Robotics Laboratory of the Instituto Tecnológico de Aeronáutica. It examines the measurement, communication, and execution conditions that underpin closed-loop flight.

Subsystem measurements are paired with integrated tests, from static marker screening to simultaneous flights with six physical drones.

Read the study abstract +

This study experimentally characterizes an indoor multi-drone testbed, addressing motion capture, radio communication, execution timing, and flight performance. The experimental approach combined subsystem-level measurements with integrated flight tests to assess measurement stability, communication performance, and closed-loop operation. The marker configuration favored by static orientation screening did not provide the largest operational flight volume. Long-duration measurements yielded a mean final estimation-error drift of 1.654 mm, while calibration repeatability tests showed a global RMS deviation of 2.859 mm. Standalone radio tests met the prescribed timing criteria for up to six drones using a single radio, but this performance did not extend to the complete off-board control loop. During simultaneous flights with six drones, reference tracking was achieved at effective loop rates of approximately 24–27 Hz, compared with the requested 100 Hz. Mean single-drone tracking RMSE ranged from 1.621 to 7.262 cm across the evaluated maneuvers, while mean session RMSE in multi-drone flights ranged from 1.697 to 7.335 cm. These findings document the testbed’s operating conditions and limitations, providing an experimental basis for planning and interpreting subsequent indoor multi-drone experiments.
